\subsection{Recoverability: when is \texorpdfstring{$u=O(1)$}{u=O(1)}?}\label{sec:recoverability}

\Cref{thm:pdl} controls on-policy compounding through one worst-case number, $u$. This subsection shows that,
for full-support teachers, $u$ is the right worst-case constant. It then replaces $u$ by averages that can be
measured, and gives conditions on the teacher under which $u=O(1)$. The conditions are stated through the
teacher's value functions and the mixing of a Markov chain that it induces. Costs, $Q^p_t$ and $V^p_t$ are as in
\cref{sec:shift}. The \emph{local recoverability}
\[
u_t(s):=\max_aQ^p_t(s,a)-\min_aQ^p_t(s,a)=\max_aA^p_t(s,a)-\min_aA^p_t(s,a),\qquad A^p_t:=Q^p_t-V^p_t,
\]
is the range of the teacher's advantage at $s$, and $u=\max_t\sup_su_t(s)$. Let
$\TV_t(s):=\TV(p_t(\cdot\mid s),q_t(\cdot\mid s))$ and $\Delta_t(s):=\sum_a\big(q_t(a\mid s)-p_t(a\mid s)\big)Q^p_t(s,a)$. By
\cref{thm:pdl}, $\Delta J:=J_c(q_\theta)-J_c(p)=\sum_t\E_{\dstud t}\Delta_t$. Let $\bar\nu^\theta$ (resp.\ $\bar\nu^p$) be the law of $(t,s_t)$
when $t$ is uniform on $\{1,\dots,T\}$ and $s_t\sim\dstud t$ (resp.\ $s_t\sim\dteach t$). Inside expectations over $(t,s_t)$ we
drop the arguments, writing $u$, $\TV$ and $\Delta$ for $u_t(s_t)$, $\TV_t(s_t)$ and $\Delta_t(s_t)$. Then $\eon^{\TV}=\E_{\bar\nu^\theta}\TV$.

\begin{theorem}[Recoverability where the student errs]\label{thm:local-u}
\Pv\Ck The following hold for every autoregressive student. Ratios $0/0$ are read as $0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item \emph{Localisation.} $\Delta J=\bar a_{\mathrm{on}}\,T\eon^{\TV}$ and
$|\Delta J|\le\sum_t\E_{\dstud t}[u_t\TV_t]=\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}\,T\eon^{\TV}$, where
\[
\bar a_{\mathrm{on}}:=\frac{\E_{\bar\nu^\theta}\Delta}{\E_{\bar\nu^\theta}\TV},\qquad
\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}:=\frac{\E_{\bar\nu^\theta}[u\,\TV]}{\E_{\bar\nu^\theta}\TV}\le u,\qquad
|\bar a_{\mathrm{on}}|\le\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}.
\]
So $\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}$ is the recoverability on the student's own prefixes, weighted by where the student errs.
\item \emph{Moments.} For $\varrho\in[1,\infty]$ and $\frac1\varrho+\frac1{\varrho'}=1$,
\[
|\Delta J|\le T\,\|u\|_{L^\varrho(\bar\nu^\theta)}\,\|\TV\|_{L^{\varrho'}(\bar\nu^\theta)}\le T\,\|u\|_{L^\varrho(\bar\nu^\theta)}\,(\eon^{\TV})^{1-1/\varrho}.
\]
\item \emph{Commitment states.} Fix $U\ge0$ and sets $B_t\supseteq\{s:u_t(s)>U\}$. Let
$\eps^B_{\mathrm{on}}:=\frac1T\sum_t\E_{\dstud t}[\TV_t\ind_{B_t}]$ be the on-policy error made on them, and
$\zeta:=\eps^B_{\mathrm{on}}/\eon^{\TV}$. Then
\[
|\Delta J|\le U\,T\eon^{\TV}+\sum_{t=1}^T(T-t+1)\,\E_{\dstud t}[\TV_t\ind_{B_t}]\le(U+\zeta T)\,T\eon^{\TV}.
\]
\item \emph{Sharpness.} Let $u_\cD:=\max_t\sup\{u_t(s):s=(x,y_{<t}),\ \cD(x)>0\}\le u$. \Cref{thm:pdl} holds with $u_\cD$ in
place of $u$. If every $p_t(\cdot\mid s)$ has full support, no smaller constant does:
$\sup|\Delta J|/(T\eon^{\TV})=u_\cD$ over autoregressive students with $\eon^{\TV}>0$. For each $(t,s)$ the ratio $u_t(s)$ is
attained by students with arbitrarily small $\eon^{\TV}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) For any $f$ and laws $p,q$, $|\E_qf-\E_pf|\le\TV(p,q)(\max f-\min f)$, so $|\Delta_t|\le u_t\TV_t$. Sum over $t$, and
note that $\sum_t\E_{\dstud t}h_t=T\,\E_{\bar\nu^\theta}h$.
(ii) Hölder's inequality. For $\varrho'<\infty$, $\TV^{\varrho'}\le\TV$ because $\TV\le1$. For $\varrho'=\infty$, use $\|\TV\|_\infty\le1$ and
$0^0:=1$.
(iii) Split each expectation on $B_t$, and use $u_t\le T-t+1$, which holds because $Q^p_t\in[0,T-t+1]$.
(iv) See \cref{app:recoverability}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
By (iv), for a full-support teacher no bound that holds for every student can improve on $u_\cD$, even for
students that are nearly lossless. Without full support the supremum can be smaller, because reaching a
state that the teacher never visits costs the student error. For the deterministic teacher of
\cref{prop:trap} below and $T\ge3$, it is $T/2<u_\cD=T-1$ (\cref{app:recoverability}). Any improvement must use
\emph{where} the student errs, as (i)--(iii) do. Part (iii) makes \cref{rem:onpolicy} quantitative. The guarantee is
linear in $T$ if, for some $U=O(1)$, the fraction $\zeta$ of the error made at commitment states is $O(1/T)$. A fixed
fraction $\zeta$ weakens it to quadratic. This is only an upper bound, since an error at a commitment state that
swaps tokens of equal value costs nothing. But the quadratic rate does occur. The average in (i) must be
taken over the student's prefixes, and an average over the teacher's prefixes does not suffice:

\begin{proposition}[Teacher-side averages do not suffice]\label{prop:trap}
\Pv\Ck Let $\cV=\{0,1\}$, and let the prefix determine a latent state $z_t\in\{\mathsf{ok},\mathsf{drift},\mathsf{sink}\}$. Start at
$z_1=\mathsf{ok}$. At $\mathsf{ok}$, token $0$ stays and token $1$ moves to $\mathsf{drift}$. At $\mathsf{drift}$, token $0$ returns to $\mathsf{ok}$
and token $1$ moves to $\mathsf{sink}$, which is absorbing. Step $t$ costs $\ind\{z_{t+1}\ne\mathsf{ok}\}$. The teacher always
emits $0$. The student emits $1$ with probability $\eps\in(0,1]$ at $\mathsf{ok}$, always at $\mathsf{drift}$, and never at $\mathsf{sink}$.
Then $u_t=1$ at every prefix the teacher visits, so $\E_{\dteach t}u_t=1$ for all $t$ and $\E_{\bar\nu^p}[u\,\TV]/\E_{\bar\nu^p}\TV=1$. But
$u_t=T-t+1$ at $\mathsf{drift}$. Moreover $\eoff^{\TV}=\eps$ and
\[
\Delta J=\sum_{t=1}^T\big[1-(1-\eps)^t\big],\qquad T\eon^{\TV}=2-(1-\eps)^T-(1-\eps)^{T-1}.
\]
If $T\eps\le1$, then $\Delta J\ge\frac{T+1}8\,T\eon^{\TV}$, so $\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}\ge\frac{T+1}8$. By continuity, the teacher-side averages and
the lower bound on $\Delta J$ carry over, up to factors arbitrarily close to $1$, to a full-support teacher that
emits $1$ with small enough probability.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
See \cref{app:recoverability}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The student never learned to leave $\mathsf{drift}$, a state the teacher never visits. This is exposure bias in its
purest form. Recoverability must therefore hold on the states that the student reaches. The next theorem
gives conditions on the teacher that guarantee this uniformly.

\begin{theorem}[Recoverability from forgetting]\label{thm:forgetting}
\Pv\Ck Each part relates $u$ to a property of the teacher alone.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item \emph{Value-function form.} For $k>t$ let $V^{(k)}_{t+1}(s'):=\E_p[c_k(s_k,y_k)\mid s_{t+1}=s']$ be the teacher's value
function for the single check $c_k$. Let $\iota_{t,k}(s):=\max_{a,a'}|V^{(k)}_{t+1}(s\,a)-V^{(k)}_{t+1}(s\,a')|$ be the largest
influence of the token emitted at $s$ on that value. Then
$u_t(s)\le\max_{a,a'}|c_t(s,a)-c_t(s,a')|+\sum_{k>t}\iota_{t,k}(s)$, and hence $u\le1+\max_t\sup_s\sum_{k>t}\iota_{t,k}(s)$.
\item \emph{Forgetting and coupling.} If $c_k(s_k,y_k)$ is a function of some statistic of $s_{k+1}$, then $\iota_{t,k}(s)$ is at
most the largest total variation between the teacher's laws of that statistic started from $s\,a$ and from
$s\,a'$. For any coupling of the teacher's continuations from $s\,a$ and $s\,a'$,
$|Q^p_t(s,a)-Q^p_t(s,a')|\le|c_t(s,a)-c_t(s,a')|+\E N$, where $N$ counts the steps $k>t$ at which the two
continuations incur different costs.
\item \emph{Mixing of a cost-sufficient chain.} Let $\cS_{T+1}:=\cX\times\cV^T$. Suppose there are maps $\phi_t:\cS_t\to\cZ_t$ for
$t\le T+1$, with $\cZ_t$ countable, and $f_t$ with $\phi_{t+1}(s\,a)=f_t(\phi_t(s),a)$ for $t\le T$. Suppose that $p_t(\cdot\mid s)$ and
$c_t(s,\cdot)$ depend on $s$ only through $z=\phi_t(s)$. The teacher then induces a Markov chain on the latent states,
with kernels $K_t(z,z'):=\sum_ap_t(a\mid z)\ind\{f_t(z,a)=z'\}$. Let $\delta(K):=\sup_{z,z'}\TV(K(z,\cdot),K(z',\cdot))$ be
Dobrushin's contraction coefficient, and $\delta_m:=\max_{t\le T-m+1}\delta(K_t\cdots K_{t+m-1})$ for $1\le m\le T$. For every such $m$
with $\delta_m<1$,
\[
u\le1+\frac m{1-\delta_m}.
\]
If $c_t(s,a)$ depends on $(s,a)$ only through $\phi_{t+1}(s\,a)$, the cost of a step is a function of the latent state
it leads to, and then $u\le m/(1-\delta_m)$. Consequently $u\le1+2m_{1/2}$ whenever $m_{1/2}:=\min\{m\le T:\delta_m\le\frac12\}$
exists. For a time-homogeneous chain with mixing time $t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\frac14)$, $u\le1+2t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\frac14)$. Under a Doeblin
condition, $K_t\cdots K_{t+m-1}(z,\cdot)\ge\alpha\,\omega_t$ for all $t$ and $z$ and some probability laws $\omega_t$, one has
$\delta_m\le1-\alpha$ and $u\le1+m/\alpha$.
\item \emph{The prefix chain itself never mixes.} For $\phi_t=\mathrm{id}$, $\delta(K_t\cdots K_{t+m-1})=1$ whenever $\cS_t$ has at least
two elements, because distinct prefixes have disjoint continuations. So $\delta_m=1$ for every $m<T$ when $V\ge2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) $Q^p_t(s,a)=c_t(s,a)+\sum_{k>t}V^{(k)}_{t+1}(s\,a)$, and the range of a sum is at most the sum of the ranges.
(ii) A function with values in $[0,1]$ changes its mean by at most the total variation. Under a coupling,
$Q^p_t(s,a)-Q^p_t(s,a')$ equals $c_t(s,a)-c_t(s,a')$ plus the expected sum of the later cost differences. Each
difference lies in $[-1,1]$ and vanishes unless $N$ counts it.
(iii) See \cref{app:recoverability}.
(iv) $K_t\cdots K_{t+m-1}(s,\cdot)$ is supported on extensions of $s$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Part (i) says that $u$ is small when no single token moves the teacher's probability of failing a later
check by much, summed over the later checks. Part (ii) makes this measurable. Force $a$ and $a'$ at $s$, roll
the teacher out from both with shared sampling noise, and count the steps whose costs differ. Part (iii)
turns the condition into mixing. The chain must live on a \emph{cost-sufficient} summary $z$ of the prefix, for
example the state of a format or unit-test checker together with whatever the teacher's next-token law
depends on. By (iv), the prefix tree itself never mixes. Doeblin's condition says the following. From every
latent state, including erroneous ones, the latent state $m$ tokens later is drawn, with probability at least
$\alpha$, from a law $\omega_t$ that does not depend on where the chain started. This is \emph{self-correction}: with
probability $\alpha$ per block of $m$ tokens the teacher forgets its errors, and $u-1$ is at most $m/\alpha$, which
bounds the expected time that forgetting takes.

\begin{example}[Commitment versus self-correction]\label{ex:selfcorrect}
\Pv\Ck Take two latent states, $\mathsf{ok}$ and $\mathsf{err}$, and let each step that ends in $\mathsf{err}$ cost $1$. From $\mathsf{ok}$ the
teacher moves to $\mathsf{err}$ with probability $\eta_{\mathrm{err}}$. From $\mathsf{err}$ it repairs, moving to $\mathsf{ok}$, with probability
$\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}$. Let $\eta_{\mathrm{err}}+\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}\le1$, and let some token lead from each state to each state. Then
$\delta_1=\lambda_2:=1-\eta_{\mathrm{err}}-\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}$, the second eigenvalue of $K$, and Doeblin's condition holds with $m=1$
and $\alpha=\eta_{\mathrm{err}}+\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}$. Moreover
\[
u=\frac{1-\lambda_2^T}{1-\lambda_2}\ \nearrow\ \frac1{\eta_{\mathrm{err}}+\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}}\qquad(T\to\infty).
\]
So the bound of \cref{thm:forgetting}(iii) for costs that depend only on the next latent state is attained in
the limit. There $u$ equals the relaxation time $1/(1-\lambda_2)$ of the teacher's error process, close to the
expected repair time $1/\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}$ when $\eta_{\mathrm{err}}\ll\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}$. Without repair and without teacher errors
($\eta_{\mathrm{err}}=\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}=0$), $\mathsf{err}$ is absorbing for the teacher, as the off-track states are in the cliff of
\cref{thm:quadratic}. Then $\delta_m=1$ for all $m$, and $u=T$.
\end{example}
\begin{proof}[Computation]
Let $\bar W_j(z)$ be the expected cost of the step that ends in $z$ at time $j$, plus that of all later steps. Then
$u_t(s)=D_{t+1}:=\bar W_{t+1}(\mathsf{err})-\bar W_{t+1}(\mathsf{ok})$, with $D_{T+1}=1$ and $D_{t+1}=1+\lambda_2D_{t+2}$. So
$D_{t+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{T-t}\lambda_2^j$, which is largest at $t=1$. The best Doeblin constant for $m=1$ is
$\sum_{z'}\min_zK(z,z')=\min(1-\eta_{\mathrm{err}},\eta_{\mathrm{rep}})+\min(\eta_{\mathrm{err}},1-\eta_{\mathrm{rep}})=\eta_{\mathrm{err}}+\eta_{\mathrm{rep}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The value martingale, and a teacher-side bound]\label{prop:valmart}
\Pv\Ck Let $\cC:=\sum_{t=1}^Tc_t(s_t,y_t)$ be the total cost, and $\Var_p(\cC):=\E_{x\sim\cD}\Var_{y\sim\Pseq}(\cC)$ its average
conditional variance under the teacher. Let $\sigma_t^2(s):=\Var_{a\sim p_t(\cdot\mid s)}Q^p_t(s,a)\le u_t(s)^2/4$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item Under the teacher, $G_t:=\sum_{k<t}c_k(s_k,y_k)+V^p_t(s_t)$ is the Doob martingale of $\cC$. Its increments are
$A^p_t(s_t,y_t)$, so
\[
\Var_p(\cC)=\sum_{t=1}^T\E_{\dteach t}\sigma_t^2=T\,\E_{\bar\nu^p}\sigma^2 .
\]
\item For a terminal reward $r$ (costs $0$ before step $T$, and $c_T=1-r$), $Q^p_t(s,a)=1-\E_p[r\mid s_{t+1}=s\,a]$. So $u_t(s)$
is the largest one-token swing of the teacher's expected reward, which is its success probability for
$0/1$ rewards. Hence $u\le1$ and $|J_r(p)-J_r(q_\theta)|\le\sum_t\E_{\dstud t}[u_t\TV_t]\le T\eon^{\TV}$. Also
$\Var_p(\cC)\le\E_x[\bar r(x)(1-\bar r(x))]\le\frac14$, where $\bar r(x):=\E_p[r\mid x]$.
\item If $\E_x\chi^2(\Qseq\|\Pseq)<\infty$, then $|\Delta J|\le\sqrt{\E_x\chi^2(\Qseq\|\Pseq)\,\Var_p(\cC)}$. If
$\chi^2(q_t(\cdot\mid s)\|p_t(\cdot\mid s))\le\chi^2_\infty<\infty$ for all $t$ and $s$, then $\E_x\chi^2(\Qseq\|\Pseq)\le(1+\chi^2_\infty)^T-1$. If
moreover $T\chi^2_\infty\le1$, then
\[
|\Delta J|\le T\sqrt{2\chi^2_\infty\,\E_{\bar\nu^p}\sigma^2}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) $G_{t+1}-G_t=c_t(s_t,y_t)+V^p_{t+1}(s_{t+1})-V^p_t(s_t)=A^p_t(s_t,y_t)$. Given $s_t$, it has mean $0$ and variance
$\sigma_t^2(s_t)$. Also $G_1=\E_p[\cC\mid x]$ and $G_{T+1}=\cC$, and martingale increments are orthogonal. The bound
$\sigma_t\le u_t/2$ is Popoviciu's inequality.
(ii) The formula for $Q^p_t$ is the definition; then apply \cref{thm:local-u}(i). Moreover
$\Var(r\mid x)\le\bar r(1-\bar r)$ because $r^2\le r$.
(iii) Finiteness gives $\Qseq\ll\Pseq$ for $\cD$-almost every $x$. For such $x$,
$\E_{\Qseq}\cC-\E_{\Pseq}\cC=\E_{\Pseq}\big[(\Qseq/\Pseq-1)(\cC-\E_{\Pseq}\cC)\big]$. Apply Cauchy--Schwarz, and then again over $x$.
Summing out the last token gives $\sum_y\Qseq(y)^2/\Pseq(y)\le(1+\chi^2_\infty)\sum_{y_{<T}}\Qseq(y_{<T})^2/\Pseq(y_{<T})$; induct on $T$.
Finally, $(1+\chi^2_\infty)^T-1\le e^{T\chi^2_\infty}-1\le2T\chi^2_\infty$ when $T\chi^2_\infty\le1$, and $\Var_p(\cC)=T\,\E_{\bar\nu^p}\sigma^2$ by (i).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the average is taken]\label{rem:where-avg}
The results above settle when $u$ can be replaced by an average.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.27\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.22\linewidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.43\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
recoverability measured & student's error & bound on $|\Delta J|$\\\midrule
worst case, $u$ & on-policy mean, $\eon^{\TV}$ & $uT\eon^{\TV}$; sharp for full-support teachers (\cref{thm:local-u}(iv))\\
student's prefixes, $\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}$ & on-policy mean & $\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}T\eon^{\TV}$ (\cref{thm:local-u}(i))\\
teacher's prefixes, $\E_{\bar\nu^p}\sigma^2$ & uniform, $\chi^2_\infty$ & $T\sqrt{2\chi^2_\infty\E_{\bar\nu^p}\sigma^2}$ if $T\chi^2_\infty\le1$ (\cref{prop:valmart})\\
teacher's prefixes & on-policy mean & none (\cref{prop:trap})\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A teacher-side average suffices when the student is accurate everywhere, including at states the teacher
avoids (\cref{prop:valmart}). Accuracy on the teacher's own prefixes is not enough (\cref{prop:trap}). The
teacher-side quantity is $\Var_p(\cC)$, the average over inputs of the variance of the teacher's total cost. It can
be estimated from the teacher alone, with at least two samples per input. $\Var_p(\cC)=O(T)$ is a
weak-dependence condition on the teacher's cost process. It holds whenever $u=O(1)$, because
$\Var_p(\cC)\le Tu^2/4$. By contrast, an absorbing error that the teacher enters with probability of order $1/T$ per
step makes $\Var_p(\cC)$ of order $T^2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Turning the bound into a prediction]\label{rem:measure-u}
$\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}$ and $\bar a_{\mathrm{on}}$ can be estimated from the two models and teacher roll-outs. Sample $y\sim\Qseq$, compute $\TV_t(s_t)$
exactly at every position, and let $S:=\sum_{t'}\TV_{t'}(s_{t'})$. If $S=0$, both estimates below are $0$. Otherwise draw
one position $t$ with probability $\TV_t(s_t)/S$. Then $S\,u_t(s_t)$ is unbiased for $\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}T\eon^{\TV}$, and $S\,\Delta_t(s_t)/\TV_t(s_t)$
is unbiased for $\Delta J$. At the drawn $s_t$, estimate $Q^p_t(s_t,a)$ for the $k$ tokens that carry most of the mass of
$|q_t-p_t|$, from $n$ fresh teacher continuations each. Given the drawn position, Hoeffding's inequality and a
union bound put all $k$ estimates within $(T-t+1)\sqrt{\log(2k/\delta)/(2n)}$ with probability at least $1-\delta$. Let
$\operatorname{tail}_t$ be the mass of $|q_t-p_t|$ on the other tokens. Then $|\Delta_t|$ is at most $\TV_t$ times the range of
$Q^p_t(s_t,\cdot)$ over the $k$ tokens, plus $(T-t+1)\operatorname{tail}_t$. So that range can replace $u_t(s_t)$ at the cost of the
residual, and the same roll-outs estimate $\Delta_t(s_t)$ up to the Monte Carlo error plus $(T-t+1)\operatorname{tail}_t$. By
\cref{thm:local-u}(i), $\Delta J=\bar a_{\mathrm{on}}T\eon^{\TV}$ exactly, so these estimates predict $\Delta J$ and its growth with $T$. The
guarantee stays linear in $T$ while $\bar u_{\mathrm{on}}$ stays bounded. It can become quadratic only if the commitment
fraction $\zeta$ of \cref{thm:local-u}(iii) stays bounded away from $0$.
\end{remark}
